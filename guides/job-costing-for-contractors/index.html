Job costing for contractors: find every job that lost money

The exact AI rule to run on your own jobs on the 1st of every month and spot the ones that lost money.

By Blue WalkerNovember 20, 2026Updated November 24, 2026

1. Pull two exports for last month

  • Invoices: one line per finished job with the quoted price and every change order, and whether each change order was billed.
  • Time sheets: crew hours per job.

Your job software and your books have both. Most job software and QuickBooks can export these as a spreadsheet. The menu names change from tool to tool, so search your tool’s help for “job costing” or “time sheet export”.

You also need materials and subcontractor cost per job. If your bills and receipts are tagged to jobs in your books, pull those too. If not, start tagging them this month.

2. Set your loaded labor rate once

Your crew costs you more than their hourly wage. The loaded rate is what one crew hour really costs you.

Take last year’s total crew cost: wages, payroll taxes, workers comp, health and other benefits, paid time off. Divide it by the crew hours that landed on jobs in your time sheets.

Example: $340,000 of crew cost over 5,000 job hours is $68 an hour.

Check it once a year, or when you give raises.

3. One sheet, one row per job

Job Quoted Change orders billed Change orders NOT billed Crew hours Materials Subs

Then add these columns as formulas, so the sheet does the math and nobody guesses:

  • Billed = Quoted + Change orders billed
  • Cost = Crew hours x your loaded rate + Materials + Subs
  • Margin = (Billed minus Cost) / Billed

If you can, add Estimated crew hours, Estimated materials and Estimated subs from your quote. That is how the AI can say why a job missed.

4. Give AI your margin and the rule

Paste the sheet into Claude or ChatGPT with this:

I own a remodeling company. Below is last month's job sheet. One row per finished job.
My loaded labor rate is $68 an hour. My target gross margin is 30%.

My rule: Flag every job under my margin. Say why.

Do the math with code, not in your head, using exactly these formulas:
Billed = Quoted + change orders billed
Cost = crew hours x $68 + materials + subs
Margin = (Billed minus Cost) / Billed

Then:
1. Show me a table of every job: job, billed, cost, margin. Sort it lowest margin first.
2. For every job under 30%, write one plain sentence on why it missed. Lead with the biggest cause, like crew hours over the estimate, materials over, a sub that ran over, or a change order that was never billed. Use dollar figures from the table only.
3. For each of those jobs, write one fix I can use on the next job. One short sentence that starts with a verb.
4. List every change order that was never billed, with the job and the amount.
5. End with one line on what the missed jobs have in common.

Plain words. No jargon. Do not make up any number that is not in my sheet or your table.

Change $68 and 30% to your own numbers, and “remodeling” to your trade.

5. Every month, before you price the next job

Run it on the 1st for last month. Read the red jobs first. Take the fixes into your next quote: hours you always miss, change orders you forget to bill, subs with no cap.

Sample numbers used in this example

All DEMO numbers: a made up remodeler with 14 jobs in October. 3 came in under 30%, and all 3 lost money:

  • Bathroom, Millbrook. Quoted $18,400. Crew hours double the estimate (220 against 110). Tile change order never billed: $1,250. Margin minus 9.9%.
  • Basement bathroom, Riverton. The plumber hit rock and billed time and materials with no cap: subs $6,200 over. Margin minus 1.6%.
  • Mudroom, Brookfield. The crew built a bench, cubbies and trim the client asked for, with no change order. Margin minus 3.7%.
