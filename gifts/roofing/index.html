7 places to put AI in a roofing company, and how to set each one up

Each one runs on every call, every quote or every job by itself. For each: what it does, the simple setup, and the rule that keeps it safe.

Before you start

Pick one. Set it up, watch it for two weeks, then do the next one. Start with number 1. The money is in the leads you already paid for.

You don’t need a new CRM. Everything here sits on top of what you use now (AccuLynx, JobNimbus, Roofr, Jobber or a spreadsheet), plus a business texting app like Quo and Claude or ChatGPT. Zapier connects them when they don’t talk to each other.

1. Every open quote followed up

What it does

Every estimate that didn’t sign gets a short follow up about that homeowner’s actual roof, on day 2, day 7 and day 21, then a check in each season. A lot of quotes don’t die on price. They die because nobody calls back. It stops the moment they reply, and your rep takes over.

Set it up

  1. Export every unsigned estimate from the last 12 months: first name, the address’s town, the job (full replacement, repair, gutters), the price, the date, and the rep’s notes.
  2. Paste the list into Claude or ChatGPT with the rules from our QUOTES gift, and say:
These are roofing estimates. Write the day 2 text for each one. Use one real detail from the notes, like the roof type, a leak they mentioned, the color they liked or a question about financing.
  1. Send from your business texting app. On Jobber, Zapier can do it by itself: trigger “Quote Sent”, wait 2 days, AI step, send.

January and February are slow in cold markets. That’s the best time to work old quotes.

Keep it safe

  • Only text people who asked you for an estimate.
  • Never change the price, never invent a deadline or a “sale”.
  • Read the first batch before anything goes out.

2. Missed calls answered in seconds

What it does

A lot of calls get missed in the middle of the day, while you’re on a roof. Every missed call gets a text back in seconds, and AI reads the reply. Water coming in goes straight to your phone.

Set it up

  • Quo (OpenPhone): Settings, Phone Numbers, your number, Automations, Auto-Replies. Turn on missed calls.
  • Jobber Receptionist texts callers who hang up, and has trigger words that alert you.
  • Our MISSED gift has the exact text and the AI rule. For roofing, the emergency list is:
Emergency: water coming in through the ceiling or walls, a tree or limb on the roof, part of the roof torn off or open to the weather.

Keep it safe

  • Text first. Homeowners hate fake voices on the phone.
  • If an AI ever answers, it says it’s the office assistant.
  • Only text people who called you, and honor STOP.

3. Storm and hail mode

What it does

When a hail storm or a big wind storm hits your area, AI checks which of your past customers and open quotes are in the hit zip codes. They get a short note offering a free roof check, and your calendar opens extra inspection slots for the next few days. Your reps get a list sorted by zip code instead of guessing.

Set it up

  1. After a storm, check the free NOAA storm reports for hail and wind in your area, and write down the zip codes.
  2. Export your past customers and open quotes with their zip codes.
  3. Paste both into Claude or ChatGPT:
Here are the zip codes hit by the storm on [date], and my list of past customers and open quotes.
Find everyone in those zip codes.
For each one, write a short, calm text from my roofing company: there was a storm in their area on [date], and we can do a free roof check if they want one. One easy question about a good day.
Never say their roof is damaged. Never mention insurance or a free roof. Under 40 words.
Then sort the list by zip code so my reps can plan their day.

Keep it safe

  • Only your own past customers and people who asked you for a quote. Never cold text or AI call homeowners. US law (the TCPA) needs their OK first, and the fines are per text.
  • Never say “your roof is damaged”. You haven’t seen it.
  • Never promise insurance will pay.

4. Every job becomes a page

What it does

Every finished roof turns into a short page on your website: the town, the problem, the work, and your own before and after photos. That’s what Google and the AI answers read when someone asks for a good roofer in their town.

Set it up

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT with the job notes and photos:

Write a short page for my roofing company's website about one real job we just finished.
Use only what is in my notes and photos. Never make up a detail, a number or a quote.

Title: the service and the town, like "Roof replacement in [town]".
Then 4 short parts:
The problem: why the homeowner called, in plain words.
What we found: what we saw on the roof.
What we did: the work and the materials, in plain words.
The result: how it was left.

Under 300 words. Plain words a homeowner would use.
Never use the customer's name or house number.
List which before and after photos to use, and write one plain caption for each.

Read it, fix it, post it. Post the same thing to your Google Business Profile.

Keep it safe

  • Ask the customer before you post photos of their home.
  • One real job, one real page. Google’s spam rules go after mass made AI pages.

5. Every finished job asks for a review

What it does

The day the crew finishes, the homeowner gets a text with your Google review link. 30 days later, a second text asks if they know anyone who needs a roof looked at.

Set it up

  1. Get your review link: in your Google Business Profile, tap “Ask for reviews” and copy it.
  2. If your CRM sends review requests, turn it on.
  3. If not: Zapier, trigger “job completed” in your CRM (Housecall Pro calls it “New Completed Job”), then send these from your texting app:
Hi [first name], thanks for trusting us with your roof. If the crew did a good job, would you leave us a quick Google review? It really helps a small company like ours. [review link]

Hi [first name], it's [your name] from [company]. How's the new roof holding up? If you know anyone who needs their roof looked at, we'd take good care of them.

Keep it safe

  • Ask every customer, not only the happy ones. Asking only happy customers breaks Google’s rules.
  • Only text customers who agreed to texts, and honor STOP.

6. Neighbor postcards after every job, with the customer’s OK

What it does

When a roof is finished, the closest homes get a postcard: we just did a roof on your street. The trust of a real job next door does the selling.

Set it up

  1. Ask the homeowner first: “Is it OK if we let a few neighbors know we did your roof? We never use your name or house number.”
  2. Pick a mail tool:
    • Jobber has a PostcardMania integration for mailings around finished jobs.
    • Housecall Pro: Zapier “New Completed Job” to SendJim’s neighbor mailing.
    • Thanks.io or Lob if you want to build it yourself. Plan on about $1 a card all in.
  3. Let AI write the card from the job:
Write a short postcard for my roofing company to send to neighbors near a roof we just finished.
The job: [roof type, color, what we did].
Say we just finished a roof on [street name], and offer a free roof check. Plain, friendly, under 50 words.
Never use the customer's name or house number. Never say their roof looks bad. No fake deadlines.
  1. Start with 25 to 50 homes per job, not hundreds, and track the calls.

Keep it safe

  • Only with the customer’s OK, and never their name or house number.
  • Never say a neighbor’s roof is damaged.

7. Insurance paperwork, summed up in plain words

What it does

AI reads the adjuster’s estimate and your photos, and writes two things: a plain summary for the homeowner of what the claim covers, and a checklist for your office of items that look missing so a person can check them.

Set it up

  1. Black out the policy number and personal details first.
  2. Paste the adjuster’s estimate and your inspection notes into Claude or ChatGPT:
Below is an insurance adjuster's estimate for a roof claim, and my inspection notes and photo list.
1. Write a plain summary for the homeowner: what the estimate covers, in words a 12 year old understands. No promises about what will be paid.
2. Write a checklist for my office: items in my inspection notes that are not in the adjuster's estimate, each with the photo that shows it. Mark each one CHECK. Do not decide anything.
Use only what is in the documents. Never make up a line item or an amount.
  1. Your supplement person or estimator checks every item before anything is sent.

Keep it safe

  • You document and request. Only a public adjuster or a lawyer can negotiate a claim for the homeowner.
  • Never promise a homeowner what insurance will pay.
  • An expert checks every one before it goes to the insurance company.

The rule for all 7

A person approves at first. Read what it writes for the first two weeks, fix what’s off, then let it run. Only text people who asked you for something, and honor STOP every time.
