7 places to put AI in an HVAC company, and how to set each one up

Each one runs on every call, every job or every customer by itself. For each: what it does, the simple setup with tools you likely have, and the rule that keeps it safe.

Before you start

Pick one. Set it up, watch it for two weeks, then do the next one. Number 5 (open estimates) is the one most shops can turn on today.

The tools below are ones you likely have already: Jobber, Housecall Pro or ServiceTitan, a business phone app like Quo, and Claude or ChatGPT. Zapier connects them when they don’t talk to each other.

1. After-hours calls answered and booked

What it does

Every call nobody picks up after hours gets answered, the problem and address get written down, and the job gets booked. No heat, a gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm goes straight to your on-call tech’s phone.

Set it up

  • Jobber: Jobber Receptionist answers and texts callers who hang up, and it has trigger words (like “emergency”) that alert you.
  • ServiceTitan: ask your account manager about its AI virtual agent for calls.
  • Any phone system: at the least, turn on missed call text back today (Quo: Automations, Auto-Replies). Our missed call guide has the exact steps.

Then give it your emergency rule. Paste this into the AI step that reads calls and texts:

You read what a caller told my HVAC company after hours. Decide if it is an emergency.

Emergency: no heat in cold weather, no cooling in extreme heat with elderly people, babies or someone sick in the home, a gas smell, a carbon monoxide alarm.
For a gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm, also tell them: leave the house now and call 911 or the gas company from outside.

Return one line only:
URGENT: <what is wrong in 5 words>, <who is at risk>, <the address>
or
NORMAL: <what they need in 5 words>

Never diagnose. Never give a price or a time.

Every URGENT line goes to the on-call tech’s cell as a text.

Keep it safe

  • It says it’s the office assistant. It never pretends to be a person.
  • Play “this call may be recorded” at the start.
  • For the first two weeks, you check every booking it makes the next morning.

2. Every recorded call checked for no-heat calls that didn’t book

What it does

AI reads every call your office did answer, and flags the ones that should have booked and didn’t. It quotes the exact line where it slipped, like “someone will call you back”.

Set it up

  • ServiceTitan: Voice Intelligence already flags bookable calls that didn’t book. Ask your account manager to turn it on.
  • Everyone else: record calls (Quo Business makes transcripts for you), then Zapier: Quo “Call Transcript Completed”, wait 5 minutes, an AI step with the rule below, then a row in a Google Sheet.
You review recorded phone calls for my HVAC company.

Flag any call where the caller had a problem we fix and did not get a booked time. Flag no-heat calls with kids or elderly people as urgent. Quote the exact line where it slipped.

For each call:
Problem: the problem in a few plain words, and who is at risk.
Bookable: yes or no.
Booked: yes only if they got a day and time, or a tech on the way.
Urgent: yes for no heat with kids or elderly people.
Where it slipped: the exact words, copied from the transcript.
What to do: one plain sentence.

Every Monday, sort the sheet by “Bookable yes, Booked no”. Our weekly routine guide has the full setup.

Keep it safe

  • About 11 to 13 US states need everyone on the call to agree to recording. Play the notice on every call.
  • Use it to fix the script, not to yell at anybody.

3. Maintenance members due get a reminder and book

What it does

Every member who is due for a tune up gets a short text, in spring for cooling and in fall for heating, and books a time. Nobody has to remember to call down the list.

Set it up

  1. Export your maintenance members with their last tune up date. Your job software can export your client list to a spreadsheet. If it can’t, keep one Google Sheet.
  2. Once a month, paste the list into Claude or ChatGPT with this:
Below is my list of maintenance members with their last tune up date.
Find everyone due for a [fall heating / spring cooling] tune up this month.
For each one, write a short text from my company: first name, that their [furnace / AC] tune up is due, and one easy question about a good day this week or next. Under 35 words. No pressure, no prices.
  1. Send them from your business texting app, with your booking link if you have one.

Keep it safe

  • Only text members who agreed to get reminders. Put it in your membership agreement.
  • Honor STOP right away.
  • Read the batch before it goes out.

4. Every system over 12 years old gets a note before winter

What it does

AI finds every furnace or heat pump in your old jobs that is over 12 years old, and the customer gets a plain note before the cold hits, with what your tech saw last time.

Set it up

  1. Pull the install year and last visit notes for each customer’s system.
    • ServiceTitan tracks installed equipment, and its Nameplate Scan fills in make, model and serial from a photo.
    • Jobber or Housecall Pro: export your jobs and notes, and add a column for the install year from your install jobs.
  2. In early fall, paste the list into Claude or ChatGPT:
Below is a list of my customers' heating systems: customer first name, system type, install year, and my tech's notes from the last visit.
Find every system that is over 12 years old.
For each one, write a short, plain note from my company: how old the system is, one thing from the tech's notes if there is one, and an offer to check it before winter.
Never say it will fail. Never scare anyone. Never give a price. Under 50 words.
  1. Send it by email, or by text to customers who agreed to texts.

Keep it safe

  • Never say a system is dangerous unless your tech wrote that down.
  • No scare words, no fake deadlines.
  • Only text people who agreed to hear from you, and honor STOP.

5. Every open estimate followed up on day 2, 7 and 21

What it does

Every estimate that hasn’t said yes gets a short follow up about that customer’s actual job, on day 2, day 7 and day 21. It stops the moment they reply.

Set it up

Turn on what you already pay for first:

  • ServiceTitan: the Virtual Agent texts unsold estimates and sends an approve link (Core, Pro and Max). Marketing Pro has an Unsold Estimates campaign. Don’t run both.
  • Jobber: Settings, Automations, quote follow ups (Connect plan and up). Two follow ups, the same message for everyone.
  • Housecall Pro: pipeline automations resend an estimate stuck in “Sent”, up to 3 times.

Then make the messages about each person’s job. Our open quotes guide has the exact rules to paste into Claude or ChatGPT.

Keep it safe

  • Only text people who asked you for an estimate.
  • Put “we may text you about your estimate” on your estimate form.
  • Never change the price, never invent a deadline.

6. Every finished job asks for a review

What it does

The day a job is done, the customer gets a text with your Google review link. Every job, not when someone remembers.

Set it up

  1. Get your review link: in your Google Business Profile, tap “Ask for reviews” and copy it.
  2. If your job software sends review requests, turn it on.
  3. If not: Zapier, trigger “job completed” in your job software (Housecall Pro calls it “New Completed Job”), then send this from your texting app:
Hi [first name], thanks for having us out today. If [tech name] did a good job, would you leave us a quick Google review? It really helps a small company like ours. [review link]

Keep it safe

  • Ask every customer, not only the happy ones. Asking only happy customers breaks Google’s rules.
  • Only text customers who agreed to texts, and honor STOP.

7. A 6am text with the whole day

What it does

At 6am you get one text: today’s jobs, who is on each one, any emergency calls from the night, estimates waiting on an answer, and anything you need to call about yourself.

Set it up

  • On Jobber: connect Jobber to Claude. It’s an official connector on every paid Jobber plan, and it can read your clients, quotes, jobs and invoices. Ask it the prompt below each morning.
  • To make it run by itself: a Zapier schedule at 6am, then today’s jobs from your job software (check that its Zapier app can look up today’s jobs; if not, have the office export tomorrow’s schedule to a Google Sheet at the end of each day), then an AI step with the prompt below, then a text to your own phone.
Write my 6am text for today. Under 600 characters. Plain words, short lines.
1. How many jobs today, and any that look tight on time.
2. Who is on each job.
3. Any emergency or no-heat calls from last night, and if each one is booked.
4. Estimates waiting on an answer for more than 2 days.
5. Anything I need to call about myself.
Use only what is in the data below. Never make anything up.

Keep it safe

  • It only goes to you.
  • No card numbers, door codes or passwords in the data you send it.

The rule for all 7

A person approves at first. Read what it writes for the first two weeks, fix what’s off, then let it run. Only text people who agreed to hear from you, and honor STOP every time.
