7 places to put AI in a plumbing company, and how to set each one up

Each one runs on every call, every job or every customer by itself. For each: what it does, the simple setup with tools you likely have, and the rule that keeps it safe.

Before you start

Pick one. Set it up, watch it for two weeks, then do the next one. Numbers 1 and 4 are the ones most shops can turn on today.

The tools below are ones you likely have already: Jobber, Housecall Pro or ServiceTitan, a business phone app like Quo, and Claude or ChatGPT. Zapier connects them when they don’t talk to each other.

1. Missed calls answered and booked

What it does

Every call nobody grabs gets a text back in seconds, or an AI that answers, gets the problem and the address, and books the job.

Set it up

Your phone app probably has the text back already:

  • Quo (OpenPhone): Settings, Phone Numbers, your number, Automations, Auto-Replies. Turn on missed calls.
  • Jobber Receptionist: answers calls, and texts callers who hang up.
  • Housecall Pro: Settings, Inbox, Voice, missed call automations.
  • Grasshopper: Instant Response.
  • ServiceTitan: ask your account manager about its AI virtual agent for calls.

The text:

Hey, it's [your company]. We're on a job and missed you. What's going on, and what's the address?

Our MISSED gift has every step.

Keep it safe

  • If an AI answers, it says it’s the office assistant. It never pretends to be a person.
  • Play “this call may be recorded” at the start.
  • Check every booking it makes for the first two weeks.

2. Burst pipe, flooding, sewer backup straight to on-call

What it does

AI reads every missed call text and every after-hours message. A burst pipe, flooding, water through a ceiling or a sewer backup goes straight to your on-call plumber’s phone, with the address. Everything else waits for the morning.

Set it up

  • Jobber Receptionist has trigger words (like “emergency” and “urgent”) that alert you. Add your own.
  • Quo plus Zapier: trigger Quo “Incoming Message Received”, then an AI step with the rule below, then if it says URGENT, a text to the on-call cell.
You read a customer's message to my plumbing company. Decide if it is an emergency.

Emergency: a burst pipe, flooding, water coming through a ceiling, a sewer backup into the home, no water at all, a gas smell.
For a gas smell, also tell them: leave the house now and call the gas company or 911 from outside.
For flooding, also tell them where the main water shutoff usually is: where the water line comes into the house, often in the basement, a crawl space or near the water heater.

Return one line only:
URGENT: <what is wrong in 5 words>, <the address if they gave one>
or
NORMAL: <what they need in 5 words>

Never diagnose. Never give a price or a time.

Keep it safe

  • Test it with 10 made up messages before it goes live, including a few that are not emergencies.
  • Only text people who contacted you, and honor STOP.

3. Water heaters over 10 years old get a note

What it does

AI finds every water heater in your old jobs that is over 10 years old, and the customer gets a plain note before it breaks. About 3 in 4 water heaters get replaced when they fail or are about to, usually by one installer with no shopping around (NEEA research). The median one is replaced at 11.3 years, and January installs run 18 to 24% above a normal month (ServiceTitan). So the note goes out in the fall, before the January rush.

Set it up

  1. Make the list: every water heater you installed or serviced, with the customer, the type and the year.
    • Export your jobs from your job software and search the notes for “water heater”.
    • From now on, have your techs snap a photo of the label on every visit. The serial number tells you the age; each brand has its own code, and building-center.org lists them.
  2. In the fall, paste the list into Claude or ChatGPT:
Below is a list of water heaters at my customers' homes: first name, tank or tankless, the year it was made or installed, and my tech's notes.
Find every one that is over 10 years old.
For each, write a short, plain note from my company: how old it is, one thing from the tech's notes if there is one, and an offer to check it before winter.
Never say it will fail or that it is dangerous. No prices. Under 50 words.
  1. Send by email, or by text to customers who agreed to texts.

Keep it safe

  • Never scare anyone. Say how old it is and offer a check, nothing more.
  • Only text people who agreed to hear from you, and honor STOP.

4. Every open estimate followed up

What it does

Every estimate that hasn’t said yes gets a short follow up about that customer’s actual job, on day 2, day 7 and day 21. It stops the moment they reply.

Set it up

Turn on what you already pay for first:

  • Jobber: Settings, Automations, quote follow ups (Connect plan and up). Two follow ups, the same message for everyone.
  • Housecall Pro: pipeline automations resend an estimate stuck in “Sent”, up to 3 times.
  • ServiceTitan: the Virtual Agent texts unsold estimates and sends an approve link (Core, Pro and Max).

Then make the messages about each person’s job. Our QUOTES gift has the exact rules to paste into Claude or ChatGPT.

Keep it safe

  • Only text people who asked you for an estimate.
  • Never change the price, never invent a deadline.

5. Every job asks for a review

What it does

The day a job is done, the customer gets a text with your Google review link.

Set it up

  1. Get your review link: in your Google Business Profile, tap “Ask for reviews” and copy it.
  2. If your job software sends review requests, turn it on.
  3. If not: Zapier, trigger “job completed” in your job software (Housecall Pro calls it “New Completed Job”), then send this from your texting app:
Hi [first name], thanks for having us out today. If [plumber name] did a good job, would you leave us a quick Google review? It really helps a small company like ours. [review link]

Keep it safe

  • Ask every customer, not only the happy ones. Asking only happy customers breaks Google’s rules.
  • Only text customers who agreed to texts, and honor STOP.

6. Every job becomes a page

What it does

Every real job turns into a short page on your website: the town, the problem, what you did, and your own photos. That’s what Google and the AI answers read when someone asks who can fix their problem.

Set it up

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT with the job notes and photos:

Write a short page for my plumbing company's website about one real job we just finished.
Use only what is in my notes and photos. Never make up a detail, a number or a quote.

Title: the service and the town, like "Burst pipe repair in [town]".
Then 4 short parts:
The problem: what the customer called about, in plain words.
What we found: what we saw when we got there.
What we did: the work, step by step.
The result: how it was left.

Under 300 words. Plain words a homeowner would use.
Never use the customer's name or house number.
List which of my photos to use, and write one plain caption for each.

Read it, fix it, post it. Post the same thing to your Google Business Profile.

Keep it safe

  • Ask the customer before you post photos of their home.
  • One real job, one real page. Google’s spam rules go after mass made AI pages.

7. Unpaid invoices get polite reminders

What it does

Every unpaid invoice gets a friendly reminder on day 7, a firm one with your pay link on day 21, and on day 35 it stops and tells you who to call.

Set it up

  • Jobber and Housecall Pro both send automatic invoice reminders (check your plan). Turn them on.
  • On QuickBooks? Claude has a ready “chase invoices” workflow built in.
  • To make each message about the actual job, our OWED gift has the exact rules to paste into Claude or ChatGPT.

Keep it safe

  • Never threaten, and never add fees or interest in a reminder.
  • You read every message before it goes out.
  • Only text customers who agreed to texts, and honor STOP.

The rule for all 7

A person approves at first. Read what it writes for the first two weeks, fix what’s off, then let it run. Only text people who agreed to hear from you, and honor STOP every time.
