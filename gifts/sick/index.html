The 6am call-out setup

A cleaner texts in sick, the right people get a shift offer, the first yes gets it, and the client hears who is coming. You stay asleep.

Before anything: only text people who said yes

Only send shift offers to crew who agreed to get shift texts. Ask each person once, in writing: “Can we text you open shifts? Reply STOP any time.” Mark it on the crew sheet. Anyone who says STOP comes off the list that day.

Step 1. The crew sheet

One row per cleaner. A Google Sheet is fine.

Column What goes in it
Name First and last name
Phone Their cell
Areas The towns they work, like “Brookfield, Oak Hollow”
Buildings they know The sites they have cleaned before
Key or alarm code on file Just yes or no for each building. Never put the code itself in the sheet or in a text.
Today’s bookings Site, start, end. Most scheduling apps export this.
Shift texts yes or no. Only “yes” gets offers.
Off today yes or no

Step 2. The rule

When a sick text comes in, the AI finds the person and their open shifts. Then the rule picks who to text. A cleaner gets the offer only if all of these are true:

  1. They are not the person who is out.
  2. They are working today (not off).
  3. They said yes to shift texts.
  4. They work that town.
  5. They know that building (for sites that need it, like an office with an alarm).
  6. They have no booking that overlaps the open shift.

Text everyone who fits at the same time. The first clean yes gets the shift. A clean yes means yes to the shift as offered: same time, no conditions. “I could do 9 not 8” is not a yes. Everyone who says yes after that gets “Thanks, it’s filled.” Then text the client who is coming.

The three messages

1. The offer (to everyone who fits)

Can you take the 8am at Brookfield Dental? 2 hours. Reply yes if you can.

Never say who is out or why.

2. Filled (to anyone who says yes after the first one, or offered a different time)

Thanks, it's filled.

And to the person who got it:

You've got it. Brookfield Dental at 8am. Thank you!

3. The client text (after the first yes)

Morning Karen! Ana's covering today. Same checklist.

Only the name of who is coming. Never who was booked, never that anyone is sick. Their health is their business.

The prompt for the AI steps

Paste this into the AI step of your automation (Claude or ChatGPT).

You help my cleaning company cover shifts when a cleaner cannot come in.

When a text comes in, I give you the crew list and today's schedule.
1. Say who sent it (match the phone number), whether they are calling out, and which of their bookings today are now open. Only their bookings.
2. Write one short shift offer: start time, place, how many hours. Like "Can you take the 8am at Brookfield Dental? 2 hours." Never say who is out or why.
3. When someone replies, say "yes" only if they can take it as offered with no change, "maybe" if they want a different time or a condition, "no" if they can't.
4. After the first yes, write the client text: who is coming, first name only, short and warm. Never mention who was booked or any health detail. Then write me one line: who is out, who is covering, and that the client was told.

No long dashes. Plain words.

Setup steps

  1. A text line for the business. Quo (OpenPhone’s new name) or Twilio. Crew texts this number, not your cell.
  2. The automation. Zapier or n8n. Trigger: a new text comes in to the business line.
  3. AI step 1: read the text with the crew sheet and today’s schedule (the prompt above).
  4. The rule: a filter or code step that checks the six rules against the sheet. Do this part as plain rules, not AI, so it never texts the wrong person.
  5. Send the offer to everyone who fits, from the business line.
  6. AI step 2: read each reply as yes, no or maybe. The first yes wins. Store “filled” in the sheet so a second yes gets “Thanks, it’s filled.”
  7. Text the client and send yourself the one-line note.
  8. Approve the first few yourself. For the first 5 to 10 call-outs, have the automation send you each text to approve before it goes out. Once it gets them right, let it run.

Cost

  • AI: about 2 cents per call-out (8 short AI steps).
  • Texts: about 1 cent each with Twilio, so about 10 cents for a call-out with 6 offers.
  • Quo is $15 to $35 a user a month.
  • Zapier needs a paid plan for multi-step zaps.

Rules to know

  • US texting from a business number needs 10DLC registration (Quo handles it; on Twilio plan for 1 to 2 weeks).
  • Honor STOP right away.
  • Never send key codes or alarm codes by text.
