Check who ChatGPT recommends in your town, and what to fix

The prompt from the video, how to read the sites it got its answer from, and the 3 things that run every week so you get named more.

Step 1: run the check (5 minutes)

  1. Open ChatGPT with search on.
  2. Paste this, with your trade and town:
Who are the 5 best [trade] companies in [town]? For each one, list the websites you got that from.
  1. Run it 3 times. Answers change every run, so write down who shows up every time, and the sites it used.
  2. Run the same thing in Perplexity and in Google’s AI Mode.

Want it done for you, with your Google Maps spots on a real map? The free rank check runs it in about 2 minutes.

Step 2: read the sources

The sites under each answer are where the AI got its facts. That list is your to do list.

For every site it used:

  • Are you on it? If not, get listed. Most listing sites are free to claim.
  • Is it the same name, address and phone as your Google profile? Fix any site where it isn’t. Same spelling, same phone number, every time.
  • Do you have reviews there? Reviews on the sites it reads count more than your own website.

Look for a licensing or state contractor list, the Better Business Bureau, and review sites. In one study of AI answers for roofers, plumbers and electricians, 39% cited the Better Business Bureau and 1 in 4 cited a government licensing site (8ight.AI, September 2026; they sell AI visibility work). The same study found the number of reviews mattered and the star rating didn’t: businesses with under 25 reviews got named 3.4% of the time, and 250 or more got named about 21% of the time.

Step 3: the 3 things that run every week

These run on every job, by themselves, once they’re set up.

1. Every finished job asks for a review

The day a job is done, the customer gets a text with your Google review link.

  1. Get your link: in your Google Business Profile, tap “Ask for reviews” and copy it.
  2. If your job software sends review requests, turn it on.
  3. If not: Zapier, trigger “job completed” in your job software (Housecall Pro calls it “New Completed Job”), then send the text from your texting app.

The text:

Hi [first name], thanks for having us out today. If we did a good job, would you leave us a quick Google review? It really helps a small company like ours. [review link]

Ask every customer, not only the happy ones. Asking only happy customers breaks Google’s rules. Only text customers who agreed to texts, and honor STOP.

2. Every job becomes a page

One real page per finished job on your website: the town, the problem, what you did, and your own photos. AI writes the draft from your photos and job notes. You read it and post it. Post the same thing to your Google Business Profile.

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT with the job notes and photos:

Write a short page for my [trade] company's website about one real job we just finished.
Use only what is in my notes and photos. Never make up a detail, a number or a quote.

Title: the service and the town, like "Water heater replacement in [town]".
Then 4 short parts:
The problem: what the customer called about, in plain words.
What we found: what we saw when we got there.
What we did: the work, step by step, in plain words.
The result: how it was left.

Under 300 words. Plain words a homeowner would use.
Never use the customer's name or house number. The street name only if they said yes.
List which of my photos to use, and write one plain caption for each.

Don’t let AI pump out pages for towns you never worked in. Google’s spam rules go after mass made AI pages. One real job, one real page.

3. A weekly AI check

Every Monday, ask the same question and write down who got named.

  • Easiest: Perplexity Tasks (on the Pro plan) runs a question on a schedule and emails you the answer.
  • Or by hand: run the Step 1 prompt every Monday and keep a Google Sheet: the date, who got named, were you named, and the sites it used.

Watch the sheet, not one answer. If a new site shows up in the sources, get listed on it.

Rules

  • Nobody can promise you a spot in ChatGPT, and anyone who does is guessing. Answers change from run to run.
  • What you can do is give it every reason to name you: reviews, the same details everywhere, and real pages.
  • Google still sends most of the calls today. These same 3 things help there too.
