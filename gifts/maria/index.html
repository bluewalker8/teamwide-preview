The 20 questions to ask your office manager before she leaves

Every small company has a Maria. Ask her these this month, while she's still happy, and turn the answers into something you can ask.

How to use this

Sit down with her for an hour. Record it on your phone. Then paste the transcript into Claude or ChatGPT and ask:

Write each answer below up as a short, plain note with a clear title. Keep every name, number and rule exactly as she said it. If an answer is unclear, mark it ASK.

Customers

  1. Who are our 10 most important customers, and why?
  2. Who is picky, and what exactly do they want done differently?
  3. Who pays late, and what actually gets them to pay?
  4. Who should we never take on again, and why?
  5. Who always asks for the same tech or crew?

Money and prices

  1. How do you really price the jobs that aren’t in the price book?
  2. Which discounts do we give, to who, and why?
  3. What did we charge our 5 biggest repeat customers last time?
  4. Which jobs do we always lose money on?

Suppliers and subs

  1. Which suppliers are late, and who do you call to fix it?
  2. Which subs are good, and which ones do we avoid?
  3. Where are the accounts, logins and passwords kept? (Get the list. Store it in a password manager, never in the AI.)

How things really work

  1. What do you do every Monday, every month end, every year end?
  2. What breaks most often, and how do you fix it?
  3. What do you check before a quote goes out?
  4. Which rules do we have that aren’t written down anywhere?
  5. What do the techs always forget?

The stuff only she knows

  1. What questions do customers ask you every single week, and what do you answer?
  2. If you were gone for a month, what would go wrong first?
  3. What should I have asked you that I didn’t?

Turn it into something you can ask

  1. On Jobber? Connect it to Claude. It’s an official connector, about two minutes, on every paid Jobber plan. It can read your clients, quotes, jobs and invoices.
  2. Make a Claude Project. Add your price book, your rules, and the notes from these 20 questions.
  3. Ask it anything: “What did we charge the Kowalskis?” “Who’s our pickiest customer?”

Keep pay, HR, card numbers and passwords out of anything shared. Everyone with access to the Project sees what’s in it.
